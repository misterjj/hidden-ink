I

          
Stat blocks on your tokens

          
Right-click a monster, Add a stat block, pick your game. The stat block opens in a window you can move, resize, or fold into a row of icons.

          
    
            
  • D&D 5e: search the SRD with Open5e, paste an AideDD link, or write it by hand.
    • 
            
  • Pathfinder 2e: paste a link from Archives of Nethys. Elite and weak versions come along.
    • 
            
  • Daggerheart: paste an adversary or environment from the online SRD.
    • 
            
  • Any other game: a free-form stat block, with your own properties and abilities.
    • 
            
  • Adapt copies any stat block into a form, so you can make it your own.
    • 
          

        

        
      

    


    

      

        

          
II

          
A secret on every token

          
The innkeeper is a cultist, the paladin's sword is cursed. Write it right on the token, even a player's own: they will never know.

          
    
            
  • Right-click any token, Add a secret note.
    • 
            
  • A small envelope marks the token, on the GM's screen only.
    • 
            
  • Markdown for lists, bold and links.
    • 
          

        

        
      

    


    

      

        

          
III

          
Notes pinned to the map

          
Traps, hidden items, mechanisms: drop them where they are. Pick the Map notes tool, click the map, write.

          
    
            
  • Four types, each with its icon: trap, hidden item, mechanism, free note.
    • 
            
  • Mark as done once the players found it: the icon fades.
    • 
            
  • Titles on the map, for the GM only, and hidden in one click.
    • 
            
  • A list of the scene's notes, with search and filters.
    • 
          

        

        
      

    


    

      

        

          
IV

          
One key, kept by you

          
Your key is created with your first note and stays in your browser. No account, no server: whoever has the key reads the notes.

          
    
            
  • Copy it and keep it somewhere safe, from the settings of the Map notes tool.
    • 
            
  • Import it on another computer, or give it to a co-GM.
    • 
            
  • Lost key, lost notes: nobody can open them, not even me.
    •

Questions

          
Before you ask

        

        

          

            Do players need to install anything?
            
No. Once the GM enables the extension in the room, it works. Players simply see nothing of it.

          

          

            Can a clever player read my notes?
            
No. Their browser only receives encrypted data. Without your key, even developer tools show nothing readable. They can tell that a note exists somewhere on the map, but not what it says.

          

          

            What if I lose my key?
            
Notes encrypted with it can no longer be read. That's why the settings ask you to copy it. If it's gone, Reset the key lets you start over in the room.

          

          

            How do I share notes with a co-GM?
            
Copy your key from the settings and send it to them privately. Once they import it, you both read and write the same notes.

          

          

            Are linked stat blocks stored?
            
No. Stat blocks from Open5e or from a link are read from their site when you open them. Only the reference is encrypted, so players can't tell which monster hides behind a token.

          

          

            Which languages are available?
            
English and French. The interface follows your browser's language, and you can change it in the settings.
